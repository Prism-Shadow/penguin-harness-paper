\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:experiments}

We define two studies on GDPEVO Essential. The first compares RSI methods within \fwname. The second compares RSI gains and prompt-cache reuse across harnesses.

\subsection{Experiment 1: Cross-Method and Cross-Benchmark RSI Evaluation}
\label{sec:experiment-rsi}

GDPEVO Essential comprises six professional domains: Court Clerk Operations, Coverage Review, Engineering Portfolio Analysis, Financial Control, Investment Portfolio Management, and Service Ticket Resolution. Each domain has five consecutive training tasks that share a container and session, with a six-hour limit. Its held-out test set contains five tasks, each run in a fresh container and session with a four-hour limit.

We fix the base model to DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 at the \texttt{max} reasoning setting. The comparison includes a no-skill baseline and four RSI methods: OpenSkill, TRACE, SkillAdam, and SkillOpt. A Super Agent orchestrates each run as described in Section~\ref{sec:orchestration}. After training, we freeze each learned skill and evaluate it on the entire test set in three independent repetitions. For the overall score, we weight the six domains equally within each repetition and report the mean and sample standard deviation across the three repetitions.

\subsection{Experiment 2: Cross-Harness RSI Gains and Prompt-Cache Reuse}
\label{sec:experiment-harness}

The cross-harness protocol compares SkillOpt and SkillAdam across \fwname, Codex CLI, Claude Code, DeepSeek Harness, and Pi. Each harness runs its own no-skill baseline and uses the same base model, reasoning setting, training and test tasks, and benchmark scorers. For each RSI method, the training budget and candidate acceptance rule are fixed across harnesses. We measure held-out score gains in points relative to the baseline in the same harness.

For each test attempt, we record the score, total input tokens, cached input tokens, output tokens, and agent execution time. We compute the cache hit rate by dividing the sum of cached input tokens by the sum of all input tokens across the three repetitions. A complete test repetition covers 30 tasks. We report test cost per repetition and account for training usage separately.

\subsection{Experiment 3: Safety Preservation and Persistent Risks in Self-Improvement}
Iterative training in RSI may introduce safety risks. Prior studies have shown that self-evolving agents can exhibit unintended behavioral degradation, termed misevolution, and that experience accumulation may undermine safety even when the underlying model remains fixed~\citep{shao2026your, zhao-etal-2026-safety}. Safety failures may also persist across successive self-improvement iterations~\citep{zhang2026safetysurvive}. Furthermore, untrusted training trajectories can be incorporated into persistent skills, potentially propagating unsafe behaviors to subsequent tasks~\citep{mao2026practice, ying2026skilljack, chen2026experience}.
To systematically investigate safety risks in RSI processes supported by \fwname, we formulate three research questions:

RQ1: Safety Preservation. To what extent do agents preserve their original safety constraints and authorization boundaries throughout iterative self-improvement?

RQ2: Persistent Skill Contamination. Can untrusted training experiences be incorporated into persistent skills through RSI, leading to unsafe behaviors that persist across subsequent tasks?

RQ3: Cross-Method Safety Comparison. Under a unified evaluation protocol, do different RSI methods exhibit varying degrees of safety degradation and persistent skill contamination?
